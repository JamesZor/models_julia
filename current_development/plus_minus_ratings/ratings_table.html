Where our plus-minus ratings agree with SofaScore — and where they don't

Two ratings built from completely different evidence. SofaScore counts what a player does on the ball; RAPM only asks what happened to the scoreline while he was on the pitch. They should agree loosely, not exactly.

How to read the numbers

Every column except the raw SofaScore rating is a z-score across the 693-player pool: 0.0 is an average player, +2.0 is two standard deviations above. That puts the two rating systems on one scale. Composite averages the four RAPM targets, following the base paper's practice of blending its plus-minus variants. Gap is composite minus SofaScore — it isolates disagreement.

What the disagreements look like. RAPM is far higher than SofaScore on Falkirk's squad (Yeats, McCann, Adams, Tait) — a side that dominated the lower tiers, where a top-down rating credits everyone on the pitch. SofaScore is far higher on Ross County's midfield (Dhanda, White, Randall) and on Dan Casey — players who accumulate on-ball events for sides that concede.

Known weakness. Forwards are our least reliable position. Kyogo and Maeda make the RAPM top 20 on SofaScore z-scores of just +0.46 and +0.48, against a top-20 average of +1.51 — every defender and midfielder around them scores far higher. Goalkeepers are excluded entirely: a keeper plays almost every minute, so his plus-minus is really a comparison against his backup.

Caveat. Roughly a fifth of RAPM variance is club identity rather than the individual. Ranked within their own squads the agreement weakens but survives, at about the 60th percentile against a 50th for chance.
